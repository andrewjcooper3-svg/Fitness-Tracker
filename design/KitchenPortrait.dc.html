Sourdough starter
Nothing going yet
Two weeks of flour and water and you have a starter that lasts for years. Everything you need is below.
What you need
A starter is flour and water left to catch wild yeast. The only things that really matter are a scale, unchlorinated water, and whole-grain flour to get it going.
Whole rye flour (small bag)
Days 1-3 only. The wild yeast and bacteria live on the bran, so a whole grain starts far more reliably than white flour. Whole wheat works if rye is hard to find.
Unbleached bread flour
Everything from day 4 on. Bleached flour is chemically treated and starts sluggishly - the label has to say unbleached.
Filtered or bottled water
Chlorine in tap water suppresses exactly what you are trying to grow. Filtered is easiest; tap left uncovered overnight also works.
Digital scale (grams)
Not optional. A cup of flour varies by 20% depending on how you scoop it, and the whole thing is a ratio.
Clear straight-sided jar, ~1 quart
Straight sides so a rise is actually measurable. A wide-mouth mason jar or a big Weck is ideal. Never seal it tight - it needs to vent.
Rubber band or dry-erase marker
Mark the level at every feed. This is how you know whether it doubled, and it costs nothing.
Instant-read thermometer (optional)
For the water and the room. Temperature is the single biggest lever on timing, so it is worth knowing.
Name it
Day 1 is 50g whole rye flour and 50g water. Tap this once you have mixed it.
Getting it going
Tick each day as you do it. Days can slip - a late feed sets it back, it does not ruin it.
